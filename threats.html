Common Threats

Here are the 3 most common ways scammers try to get your money or information, how to spot them, and what to do.

Phishing Scams

What is it?

Phishing (said like "fishing") is when a scammer pretends to be someone you trust, like your bank, Medicare, the IRS, a delivery company, or even a family member. They send you an email, text message, or phone call to trick you into giving them your passwords, bank card numbers, or money.

Example of a fake bank email. The red number show the warning signs.

How to spot it

  1. Strange email address. The name says "Your Bank" but the email address is not your bank's real website.
  2. Rushing or scaring you. Words like "urgent" or "closed in 24 hours" are used so you act before you think.
  3. No Name. It says "Dear Customer" instead pf ypur real name.
  4. Asks for private information. Real companies never ask for your password or Social Security number in an email.
  5. Strange Link. Rest your mouse on a link without clicking. If the web address looks stange, do not click it.
  6. Spelling mistakes. Words like "permanant" and "Department" are big warning sign.
Remember: Only download programs and apps from the company's real website or your app store.

What to do

  • Do not click any links or open any attachments.
  • Do not reply and do not call any phone nymber in the message. (this applies for any possible threat.)
  • If you are worried, call the company using the phone number on the back of your card or on your paper statment.
  • Report phishing emails by forwording them to reportphishing@apwg.org. Forward scam text messages to 7726.
  • Then delete the message.

Malware

What is it?

Malware is short for "malicious software." It is a harmful program that gets onto your computer or phone, usually when you click a bad link, open an email attachment, or download a "free" program from a website you do not know. Once it is on your device it can steal your passwords, watch what you type, fill your screen with pop ups, or let a scammer control your computer.

Example of a fake virus warning on a website. The red numbers are warning signs.

How to spot it

  1. Stange web address. The website is not one you know or meant to visit.
  2. Scary warning out of nowhere. A web page connot really check your computer for viruses.
  3. A phone number to call. Real companies like Microsoft and Apple never put a phone number in a warning popup.
  4. "Do not close this window." This is a trick to keep you there. It is safe to close it.
  5. A download button. The "free cleaner" is the malware. Never download anything that pops up as free download unless it is from a trustable source.

Other signs your computer might have malware

  • Your computer or phone is suddenly very slow.
  • Lots of pop ups or ads showup, even when your web browser is closed.
  • New programs or toolbars show up that you did not install.
  • Your friends get strange emails or messages "from you."
Remember: Only download programs and apps from the company's real website or your app store.

What to do

  • Do not call the nuber and do not click anything inside the pop up.
  • Close your web browser. If it will not close, hold down the power button for 10 seconds to turn it off, then you can turn it back on.
  • Run a free malware scan. See Malwarebytes Free on the tools page. Windows has their Windows defender as well.
  • If you already let someone into your computer, call your bank and change your passwords from a different device.

Ransomware

What is it?

Ransomware is a type of malware that locks up your photos, documents, and other files so you cannot open them. Then the criminals show a message asking for money, usually in Bitcoin or gift cards, to unlock them. It gets onto your computer the same ways as other malware, like a bad email attachment, link, or fake download.

Example of ransomware screen. The red number show the warning signs.

How to spot it

  1. Your files are locked. Your photos and documents will not open or have strange names.
  2. Asking for money. They want Bitcoin or girft cards because it is hard to track. Real companies never ask for these.
  3. A countdown timer. This is to scare you into paying fast.
  4. Threats. They say your files will be deleted forever if you do not pay.
Do not pay! Paying does not mean you will get your files back.

What to do

  • Disconnect from the internet right away. Turn off Wi-Fi or unpug the internet cable.
  • Stop using the computer and ask a family member you trust or a computer repair shop for help.
  • Report it to the FBI at ic3.gov.

How to protect yourself

  • Male a backup copy of your photos and files on a USB drive or in the cloud (like OneDrive, Google Drive, or ICloud). If your files get locked, you still have a copy.
  • Turn on Ransomware Protection in windows. The tools page shows you how.
  • Keep your computer updated and do not open email attachments you were not expecting.
