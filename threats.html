Phishing Scams

What is it?

Phishing (said like "fishing") is when a scammer pretends to be someone you trust, like your bank, Medicare, the IRS, a delivery company, or even a family member. They send you an email, text message, or phone call to trick you into giving them your passwords, bank card numbers, or money.

Example of a fake bank email. The red number show the warning signs.

How to spot it

  1. Strange email address. The name says "Your Bank" but the email address is not your bank's real website.
  2. Rushing or scaring you. Words like "urgent" or "closed in 24 hours" are used so you act before you think.
  3. No Name. It says "Dear Customer" instead pf ypur real name.
  4. Asks for private information. Real companies never ask for your password or Social Security number in an email.
  5. Strange Link. Rest your mouse on a link without clicking. If the web address looks stange, do not click it.
  6. Spelling mistakes. Words like "permanant" and "Department" are big warning sign.
Remember: Only download programs and apps from the company's real website or your app store.

What to do

  • Do not click any links or open any attachments.
  • Do not reply and do not call any phone nymber in the message. (this applies for any possible threat.)
  • If you are worried, call the company using the phone number on the back of your card or on your paper statment.
  • Report phishing emails by forwording them to reportphishing@apwg.org. Forward scam text messages to 7726.
  • Then delete the message.

Malware

What is it?

Malware is short for "malicious software." It is a harmful program that gets onto your computer or phone, usually when you click a bad link, open an email attachment, or download a "free" program from a website you do not know. Once it is on your device it can steal your passwords, watch what you type, fill your screen with pop ups, or let a scammer control your computer.
